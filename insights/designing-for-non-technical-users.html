Designing for non-technical users

By the Kinetix Team · 5 min read

Most people will never read your documentation. They will click. Our job is to make the click obvious and the mistake easy to recover from.

Assumptions are expensive

When a tool expects users to understand a database, or to decode why a field is greyed out, it turns everyday work into a daily puzzle. Design for the person who has never heard of your data model.

Use plain language

Name buttons after actions people recognize: Save, Print, Schedule, Send. Avoid jargon. If a user’s mental model says “book the room”, the button should say “Book the room”.

Progressive disclosure

Show the essential, hide the rare. The screen used eight hours a day should fit one page; the advanced panel sits two clicks away for the people who need it.

Forgiving interfaces

Every action should be undoable, or at least confirmable. Destructive actions get a pause. Errors describe the fix, not just the problem, “card number is too short” beats “Error 402”.

Test with real users

Sit with three real people using the tool on a real task. Where they hesitate is your roadmap. Two hours of watching will save months of support calls.

Good software explains itself. Great software doesn’t need to.

Non-technical users are not less capable; they are less interested. Respect their focus and they will respect your tool, by using it every day.
