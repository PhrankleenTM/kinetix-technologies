How to choose between a custom system and off-the-shelf tools

By the Kinetix Team · 5 min read

Custom or off-the-shelf is not a religious debate. It is an engineering decision with a clear cost structure. Here is the framework we use with clients.

Start with the workflow

Draw your core work on one page: the steps, the people, the exceptions. The shape of that chart decides the answer. If exceptions are frequent and central, custom starts to win.

Take a clinic as an example. The generic system thinks in patients, appointments, and invoices — neatly separated modules. The real clinic thinks in visits: who came, what happened, what was billed, what comes next, all tangled into one flowing day. That mismatch is why so many generic systems feel like extra work. Every slot where the tool does not match the routine is a slot your team pays for twice.

When off-the-shelf wins

When the problem is common, the market is mature, and your workflow fits the standard model, buying is almost always cheaper, faster, and safer. Thousands of other users have already paid for the tool.

  • The tool covers the core well enough that you save years
  • Support, updates, and security are someone else’s job
  • Integration ecosystems are already built

When custom wins

When your workflow is the product, the reason customers choose you, standard tools force you to shape the business around them. That is the moment to build.

  • Your process repeats across branches and you need consistency
  • You need data woven across systems, not carried between exports
  • You want to own and improve the system over years

The hidden costs

Off-the-shelf software bills you in renewals, customizations, and workarounds. Custom software bills you in maintenance and evolution. Price the time horizon: the answer often flips between year one and year five.

The renewals look small until you add them up: per-seat fees, the inevitable upgrade that breaks your integration, the “premium” tier for the one report you actually need. Custom work looks expensive until you realize the tool is doing three jobs you used to pay three tools for. Neither number is right or wrong — it just has to be counted honestly over the life of the decision, not the first invoice.

A simple decision rule

  • Does the standard tool cover 80% of your core work? Buy.
  • Is your workflow a competitive advantage? Build.
  • Are exceptions everywhere? Build the core, buy the edges.
  • Unsure? Prototype both, small tests answer big questions.

The hybrid path is more common than people admit. Keep a mature tool for what it is best at, like accounting or email, and build the parts that make your business different. Most of our projects end up here, and it is usually the right answer.

Neither choice is lazy. Both can be exactly right. Decide based on the work, not the hype.
